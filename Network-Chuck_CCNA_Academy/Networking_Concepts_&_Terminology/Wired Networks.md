## Wired Networks

#### Wired Ethernet 

- A wired LAN consists of Ethernet cables, workstations, servers, office printers, switches, and routers.

##### Media Access Control

- A wired LAN utilizes the Media Access Control (MAC) address, which is a unique 48-bit address assigned to a network interface card.
- The first 24 bits of the Media Access Control address contribute to the Organizationally Unique Identifier (OUI).
- The last 24-bit address contribute to the vendor-assigned address for the NIC and Interface.
- A device can have multiple Media Access Control cards that can be plugged into the same LAN or be utilized for another network.
- When it comes to networking and delivery, it's about identifying devices and knowing where to send the frame or packet.
- The 48-bit address is represented in hexadecimal format. 

##### Ethernet Cabling

- Two types of cables when transmitting data over the network: Twisted-pair and Fiber-Optic cable
- Twisted-pair cables are made of copper lines insulated with a plastic coat to protect each wire and identify them by color, and an outer layer to protect from physical damage. Some are shielded twisted pairs, with braided foil shielding and a shield on each wire. Ethernet cables are terminated with an RJ-45 cap. The IEEE labeled the 802.3 standard number for Ethernet.

- The reason why the pairs are twisted is to prevent crosstalk. Crosstalk is noise generated by adjacent pairs.
- Also, Twisted-pairs are sensitive to Electro-Magnetic-Interference (EMI) and Radio-Frequency-Interference (RFI).
- Some copper cables cannot exceed 100 meters, or the signal will degrade.
  
##### Fiber-Optics

- Fiber-optic cable is composed of two kinds of glass: core and cladding. Fiber-optic cable is layered to protect the fiber from damage.

- Unlike copper cable, fiber-optic isn't sensitive to electromagnetic interference or radio-frequency interference. Fiber optics utilizes light pulses that get converted into electrical signals. Vice versa, electrical signals are converted into light pulses to travel long distances.
- Fiber optics has impressive distances; light pulses create a clearer signal and greater bandwidth than copper.
- Fiber optics is susceptible to sharp bends and a bad actor tapping the line. Servicing fiber optics requires a specialist to splice and terminate the cable.
