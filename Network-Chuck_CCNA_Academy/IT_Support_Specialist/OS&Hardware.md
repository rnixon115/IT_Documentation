## Basic Safety

### Electrical Safety

- Always read the OEM manual to prevent fires, injuries, and fatalities.
- Power supplies and other components can contain high voltage. Even after the device is turned off, it may still retain a dangerous level of voltage.
- Electrical devices have specific power requirements that must be met. AC adapters and power supplies are designed for a particular power output. If the power input or output exceeds the designated levels, it can damage the adapters or the electronic devices themselves.
- Electrical equipment must be properly grounded at all times to ensure safety and prevent harm.

#### Electrostatic Discharge (ESD)

- Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) can occur when static electricity builds up on one surface and then comes into contact with another surface that has a different electric charge.
- ESD can cause damage to sensitive electronic components, so it is important to take precautions to prevent it.

## Serial Connectivity

#### Legacy Ports

   1. Serial ports were once used to connect peripherals like printers and modems. Today, they are still occasionally used for console connections to configure network devices. The two main types of serial ports are the 9-pin DB-9 and the 25-pin port.nd the 25-pin port.
   2. Parallel ports have a 25-pin connector for connecting peripheral devices. They transmit multiple bits of data simultaneously, which is why they are commonly known as printer ports, as they were often used for printers.
   3. The 15-pin game port was used for joystick input, initially on dedicated game controller cards and later integrated into sound cards and PC motherboards.
   4. The PS/2 is a 6-pin DIN connector for connecting a keyboard and mouse, with purple for the keyboard and green for the mouse.
   5. Audio ports connect devices to computers, including a line-in for external sources, a microphone port, and line-out ports for speakers or headphones.

#### Video Ports & Connectors

   1. The Video Graphics Array (VGA) is an old analog port still found on some PCs but is becoming obsolete. It is blue in color and uses a 15-pin connector arranged in three rows.
   2. The Digital Visual Interface (DVI) was created with the rise of digital displays like LCD monitors and TVs for transmitting uncompressed digital video. DVI has several variants: 
   - **DVI-A** (Analog) for analog signals only  
   - **DVI-D** (Digital) for digital signals only  
   - **DVI-I** (Integrated) for both  
   There are also two types of connections: single-link, using one TMDS transmitter, and dual-link, using two TMDS transmitters for higher resolutions. The standard DVI connector is white and can have up to 24 pins for digital signals, 4 pins for analog signals, and a ground bar.
   3. High-Definition Multimedia Interface (HDMI) was designed for high-definition televisions but is also suitable for computers. It transmits video, audio, and control signals through a 19-pin connector. A smaller version, Mini-HDMI, is available for portable devices. HDMI supports high resolutions and can adjust the monitor's refresh rate to match the source output. The most common connectors are Type A (Standard), Type C (Mini), and Type D (Micro).
   4. DisplayPort is a modern technology meant to replace DVI and VGA for connecting monitors, using a 20-pin connector for high-bandwidth video and audio. There’s also a Mini DisplayPort version, mainly for Apple computers. It connects high-end PCs and displays, supporting up to 80 Gbps with versions 2.0 and 2.1. Additionally, DisplayPort can connect multiple monitors to one video source using a single cable.
   5. The USB Type-C connector is a compact, rectangular interface with rounded corners, designed to connect various peripheral devices to a PC. It serves as both USB and Thunderbolt 3, offering functionality for data transfer, video output, and power delivery through a single cable.

#### USB Cables & Connectors 

   - The Universal Serial Bus (USB) protocol has evolved significantly over the years:

      - USB 1.0 offered 1.5 Mbps for low-speed devices and 12 Mbps for full-speed.
      - USB 2.0 increased rates to 480 Mbps (High Speed).
      - USB 3.0 boosted this to 5 Gbps (SuperSpeed).
      - USB 3.1 further enhanced it to 10 Gbps.
      - USB 3.2, the latest USB-C standard, supports speeds up to 20 Gbps (SuperSpeed+). 

These advancements have made USB an essential interface for a wide range of devices.
     
   1. USB Type-A: This is a rectangular connector found on virtually every desktop PC and laptop, as well as TVs, game consoles, and media players. USB 1.1, 2.0, and 3.0 Type-A connectors and receptacles are physically compatible.
   2. USB Type-B: The USB Type-B connector is commonly used to connect printers and external hard drives. It has a square shape with beveled exterior corners and an extra notch at the top.
   3. USB Type-C: The USB Type-C connector is the newest USB interface. It is smaller than the Type-A connector and is rectangular with four rounded corners. Both Thunderbolt 3 and USB Type-C are examples of multipurpose cables that can be used to attach different kinds of peripheral devices to a PC. USB Type-C is the shape of the port. Thunderbolt 3 combines the functionality of USB, Thunderbolt, DisplayPort, and the ability to deliver power to devices through the cable.
   4. Micro-USB: The Micro-USB connector is found on smartphones, tablets, and other devices. Except for Apple, most manufacturers have adopted the Micro-USB interface. The USB 2.0 Micro-B connector has two corners pushed in at an angle.

#### Power Cables

   1. C13 to NEMA 5-15P: This is the most common type used with desktop computers.
   2. C19 to NEMA 5-15P: This is sometimes used for high-powered equipment, such as servers or large workstations. It looks like a larger version of the C13.
   3. Barrel connector: This connector is round and fits into a matching port on the laptop. Each laptop requires a specific voltage and current, so using the correct adapter, often called a “power brick”, is important.
   4. USB-C: Many newer laptops are using this instead of barrel connectors. USB-C is smaller, reversible, and can handle both power and data. It is also the most common type used with Android devices and newer iPhones and iPads.
   5. MagSafe: Some Apple laptops use this type of charger. It magnetically attaches to the laptop, making it easy to connect and helping prevent damage if the cable is pulled suddenly.
   6. Lightning cable: Apple devices like iPhones and iPads use this cable. It is thin and reversible, but it is only compatible with Apple products.
   7. Micro-USB: This cable has a smaller, tapered end, fitting only one way into the device. Older Android phones typically use this.

#### Adapters & Converters

   - **Adapter:** An adapter is a component that physically connects one technology to another. For instance, a DVI to HDMI adapter serves this purpose and can be a standalone component or a cable with different ends.
   
   - **Converter:** A converter not only connects different technologies like an adapter but also translates signals from one format to another. For example, a USB 3.0 to SATA converter enables a hard disk drive to function as a flash drive.
   
   **Examples:**
   
   1. **DVI to VGA Adapter:** This adapter connects a VGA cable to a DVI port.
   2. **USB to Ethernet Converter:** This converter changes USB signals to Ethernet signals, allowing network connectivity.
   3. **USB to PS/2 Adapter:** This adapter connects a USB keyboard or mouse to a PS/2 port.
   4. **DVI to HDMI Adapter:** This adapter connects an HDMI cable to a DVI port.
   5. **HDMI to VGA Converter:** This converter translates HDMI signals to VGA signals.

### Windows Tools Overview

   1. Task Manager
      - Task Manager can show applications running, services, who's signed in, startup apps, CPU usage, Memory usage, Wi-Fi or Ethernet usage, Disk usage, etc. Gives an overview of what's running on the computer.
      - [Task Manager for Windows 11](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/shows/inside/task-manager)
   2. Event Viewer
      - Keeps a record of everything that happens on the computer. If the computer restarted randomly without explaining why, first look at Event Viewer to see what could've caused the issue.
      - [Event Viewer for Windows 11](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/shows/inside/event-viewer)
   3. System Information
      - System Information provides an overview of the hardware, operating system version, and other applications in use. If you need to upgrade the RAM or replace another component, check here first to see the specs.
      - [System Information for Windows 11](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/description-of-microsoft-system-information-msinfo32-exe-tool)  
   4. Windows PowerShell (.ps1)
      - Windows PowerShell is a scripting language based on .net. Windows PowerShell uses an object-based {verb}-{noun} naming convention. Windows PowerShell isn't case-sensitive compared to bash or zsh.
      - [What is Windows PowerShell](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/scripting/what-is-windows-powershell?view=powershell-7.6)
          1. Get-Process
          2. Get-ChildItem
          3. Get-Printer
          4. Get-Help
          5. Get-StorageFileServer
          6. Get-StartApps
          7. Get-PnpDevice
          8. Start-Process
          9. Set-Variable
          10. Remove-Printer
          11. Remove-PrinterDriver
          12. Remove-NetSwitchTeam
          13. Remove-NetNeighbor
          14. Remove-NetIPAddress
        
   5. Command Prompt (CMD)
      - [What Is CMD?](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-server/administration/windows-commands/cmd)
      1. dir
      2. cd {directory}
      3. cd .. "Moves back one directory
